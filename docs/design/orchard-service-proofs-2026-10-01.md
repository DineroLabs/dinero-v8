Each actual WalletService now owns at most one OrchardProofJobs instance, shared by its account operations. Access is through the existing thread-affine WalletUse; the caller must retain that service lifetime owner for every access. The first access starts the existing one-worker/four-job executor once. A private implementation keeps the service class layout identical across backend ON/OFF translation units. OFF reports backend unavailable. The real core target links the proof executor and only the actual wallet_service.cpp receives its backend definition.

Jobs own their randomized plans and proof results and perform no wallet, database, chainstate or service callbacks while proving. Existing service operation owners drain before Stop joins the executor, outside the lifecycle, SQLite and selected-chain mutexes. The executor is destroyed before the wallet manager. A successful Stop/re-Init creates fresh execution ownership, and ordinary idle scope exit drains the owned worker through its destructor. Active proof computation is still nonpreemptible; this change does not establish bounded shutdown latency or forced-crash safety.

Four executed ON cases and two OFF cases cover service/backend refusal, one shared started executor, ordinary Stop/re-Init and idle scope exit with encrypted wallet preservation, and the real spend owner/proof executor/Ready/admission/mining path for transfers and unshielding. Completed proof computation neither unlocks the wallet nor persists Ready. Locked Ready refusal retains the prior reservation; the host may retain its completed result and retry after authorized unlock. Existing fixture/assertion/deadline bodies remain unchanged; no race, churn, synchronization-removal or unsafe-original controls are added.

This is execution lifetime ownership, not a public wallet-operation authorization API. The RPC host still needs request/operation binding, durable status and retry/cancellation policy, cross-session result handling and transparent-input shielding. It must authenticate the selected wallet and durable reservation, prove outside the wallet lease, commit exact Ready bytes before exposing them, and perform fresh node admission. The new component tests exercise those steps directly; no complete wallet send RPC or transport qualification is claimed. Mainnet activation remains unset; whole-node/load/platform and release gates remain open.
